# Medical safety boundary

## The app may say

- Here is what you recorded.
- Here is a timeline.
- Here is a summary you may want to discuss with a doctor or therapist.
- This may be worth bringing up with a specialist.
- This summary is based on your notes and should be reviewed before sharing.

## The app must not say

- You have a specific disorder.
- This medication caused this symptom.
- You should start, stop, increase, or reduce medication.
- This treatment is working or not working.
- You do not need a doctor.
- This is safe to ignore.
- This is medical advice.

## Medication and treatment boundary

The app records what the user says about treatment changes.

The app does not validate, recommend, or correct treatment decisions.

Medication names are optional. Users may use private labels such as:

- morning pill
- blue pill
- ABC
- ADHD med
- antidepressant
- treatment from Dr X

This improves privacy and comfort, but it does not make the data non-health data. A note about symptoms, side effects, treatment changes, mental health, or appointments must still be treated as sensitive health-related data.

## Safety interruption

If the user records possible safety-critical content, the app should not continue as if this is ordinary journaling.

Possible safety-critical content includes:

- suicidal thoughts
- self-harm urges
- sudden severe agitation
- possible mania or hypomania
- psychotic symptoms
- severe allergic reaction
- chest pain
- fainting
- dangerous palpitations
- immediate danger to self or others

The app should show a clear safety message directing the user to emergency services, crisis support, or a qualified professional.

The app must not attempt to manage emergencies itself.

## Suggested safety copy

If you may be in immediate danger, or you might hurt yourself or someone else, contact emergency services now.

If this is about suicidal thoughts, self-harm urges, severe agitation, hallucinations, chest pain, fainting, severe allergic reaction, or dangerous palpitations, this app is not the right place to handle it.

Please contact emergency services, a crisis line, or a qualified medical professional.
